Endemism-Plant Correlation Model Optimization
===============================================
Date: 2026-03-24
Status: Planning (not yet implemented)

CONTEXT
-------
The endemism-plant linear model (rawEndemismCalculator.R) is the foundation
for scaling plant endemism patterns to animal endemism. The R-squared of this
correlation directly affects confidence in the extinction projections.
Improving this correlation (without overfitting) strengthens the analysis.


PROPOSED APPROACHES
-------------------

1. Filter animal data by sampling effort (IMPLEMENTED)
   - Already available via min_occurrences parameter in run_animal_model().
   - Uses the top Nth percentile of species-rich cells to derive an
     occurrence threshold. Only cells above this threshold are used in the lm().
   - Risk: Low. Removes noisy data without altering the predictor.
   - Caveat: Aggressive thresholds reduce n; avoid n < ~20.

2. Limit taxonomic scope of plant data
   - Subset plant endemism to specific clades (e.g., angiosperms only,
     gymnosperms + angiosperms, specific families).
   - Rationale: Some animal groups may covary more strongly with specific
     plant clades (e.g., herbivorous insects with their host plant families,
     understory birds with angiosperm-dominated vegetation).
   - Risk: Moderate. This adds a degree of freedom -- the choice of clade
     must be justified a priori (ecological reasoning), not selected post-hoc
     to maximize R-squared.
   - Implementation note: Would require recomputing plant endemism from
     Stage 0/1 outputs with a taxonomic filter. The current pipeline
     computes plant endemism across all vascular plants.

3. Joint filtering of plant + animal data
   - Combine approach 1 and 2: restrict to cells with well-sampled animal
     data AND well-sampled plant data (e.g., cells above a plant occurrence
     threshold as well).
   - Risk: Highest overfitting risk. Restricting both predictor and response
     to best-case data may produce an optimistic R-squared that does not
     generalize to the cells where predictions matter most (poorly-sampled
     ones).
   - Mitigation: Use hold-out validation or cross-validation to assess
     generalizability.


MODEL COMPARISON STRATEGY
-------------------------
Rather than manually iterating over strategies and checking R-squared,
implement a systematic comparison framework:

- Define candidate strategies as a list (each specifying an animal filter
  and/or a plant taxonomic filter).
- For each strategy, fit lm(animal_endemism ~ plant_endemism) and record:
    * R-squared
    * AIC (preferred over R-squared because it penalizes model complexity)
    * n (number of grid cells used)
    * Residual diagnostics (normality, heteroscedasticity)
- Present results as a comparison table.

AIC NOTES:
- AIC is more appropriate than raw R-squared for comparing models fit on
  different subsets, because it penalizes for the effective number of
  parameters.
- However, AIC comparisons are strictly valid only when models are fit to
  the SAME response data. If subsets change which cells are included, use
  cross-validation (e.g., leave-one-out or k-fold) for a fair comparison.
- BIC is an alternative that penalizes complexity more heavily and may be
  preferable given the small n expected in most subsets.
